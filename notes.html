Where There Is No ___: A Minimum Viable Classroom

Project: IBECLP 1, Fall 2026: Mini Action Plan (PSP Environment and Learning) Context: Maglares Discovery School. The model transfers to larger schools that want a mobile lab or temporary learning space in a remote setting, for example a forest classroom where a group stays for a week or more. Focus: The logistics of setup, and what it would take a school to build one. Status: Working draft (Nov 9, 2026). The kit inventory comes first; the action plan chart comes after.

Framing

The title borrows from Hesperian's Where There Is No Doctor, a field manual that lets non-specialists act competently without the usual infrastructure. Each blank names a missing support, and the classroom is designed to keep working without it:

Where there is no… The design answer
power Solar plus battery, a power budget, low-wattage devices
internet A satellite link treated as a bonus; the classroom runs offline first
cloud A local server, local storage and local updates
walls A portable shelter, weatherproof cases, humidity control
technician Simple systems, spares, checklists, and student tech leads
screen Analog tools in parallel: notebooks, hand lenses, sketching
doctor First aid, communications and a risk plan (a nod to the original book)

Design principles

  1. Pedagogy drives the kit. Each item has to serve a step in the learning cycle. Technology sometimes reveals new affordances, so leave room to discover them, but the IB learning pathway decides what goes in the case.
  2. Justify every tool. Working with the Starlink connection is fine, but every tool has to earn its place. If the learning works without technology, it stays physical.
  3. Tiered build. MVP is the minimum for a working week. Enhanced adds depth once the MVP has proven itself. Aspirational is the full field studio. Each tier can become a phase in the action plan.
  4. Carry-in, carry-out. Everything packs into labelled, weatherproof cases that two adults can move, and the kit comes back intact.
  5. Analog redundancy. Every digital capture tool has a paper or hand-tool twin.
  6. Intentional, not low-tech. The room can be high-tech, but nothing goes on a table unless it serves that lesson. Lesson setups name the minimum kit.

The learning cycle the kit serves

Observe → Capture → Investigate → Make → Share → Reflect

This maps loosely onto PYP inquiry (units of inquiry, documentation, the Exhibition, action) and MYP inquiry, action and reflection (the design cycle, sciences investigation, the personal project, service as action). The IB pathway gets its own section later. For now, each kit item is tagged with the stage it serves.

IB frameworks that shape the kit

1. IB approach to technology

Status: a working summary from secondary sources. It is not yet verified against IB documents.

IB-authored sources to locate (ibo.org / Programme Resource Centre):

  • Teaching and learning with technology (IB, December 2015). This is the IB's own guidance, cited in the Ideal libraries bibliography, and the primary source to find.
  • IB Digital Blueprint (ibo.org/digital-blueprint). Check whether it is guidance for schools or the IB's own digital strategy before citing it.
  • IB statement of principles on AI (ibo.org news).
  • The role of ICT in the PYP (2011). This is the older PYP document, possibly superseded.

Working summary (to confirm against the sources above):

  • Technology is an integrated medium for inquiry, collaboration, ethics and human-centred problem solving, not an add-on.
  • Systems thinking and mindful innovation: responsible, data-informed technology that strengthens the educator–student relationship rather than replacing human agency.
  • Technology as a medium of inquiry: hands-on investigation, real-time data collection and independent research.
  • Global collaboration: co-creating across borders, building cultural agility.
  • Digital citizenship and ethics: media literacy, privacy, evaluating bias in sources, and an Acceptable Use Policy, developed progressively from the PYP through the MYP.
  • PYP: digital citizenship, basic coding (e.g. Scratch), digital expression, balancing screen time with hands-on inquiry.
  • MYP: media literacy, detecting bias, evaluating digital sources within subject assessment; the design cycle.
  • AI: treated as a structured assistant, not a shortcut around critical thinking; tied to academic integrity.

What this means for the kit:

  • The sensing kit and data loggers are technology as a medium of inquiry.
  • Scratch (which has an offline editor) and micro:bit suit PYP coding and the MYP design cycle.
  • A field-specific Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) covers photographing people and places, consent, location data and sharing.
  • Offline AI stays Aspirational, with use guidelines tied to the academic integrity policy.
  • The analog twins carry the screen-time balance.

Supporting research: the IB's DP technology research summary (IB Research, 2015, Cooker, Crook & Ainsworth) concludes that innovation and implementation should be driven by pedagogy. It names time, money and professional development as the main barriers. https://www.ibo.org/globalassets/new-structure/research/pdfs/technology-in-the-dp-summary-en.pdf

2. IB Ideal libraries: A guide for schools (June 2018)

Status: read in full. Page numbers below are the document's printed page numbers.

  • Definition (p. 2): the IB defines libraries as "combinations of people, places, collections and services that aid and extend learning and teaching." The library and librarian are treated as one system, the library/ian (after Tilke, 2015).
  • Places are distributed (p. 3): because libraries extend learning, they must reach every place where learning happens. Libraries need not be one central space and can be placed where the community needs them, and the guide shows a distributed physical library model (Figure 2). This is the strongest IB warrant for a library-run mobile lab or field classroom.
  • The 6+1 framework (p. 4): curating, caretaking, catalyzing, connecting, co-creating and challenging energize learning; the +1, catering (giving users only what they ask for, like an algorithm), does not. The mobile lab can be designed around the six.
  • Misinterpretations to avoid (p. 5):
  • the library as storage for uncatalogued things (a risk for a kit that sits in cases between trips)
  • fully virtual libraries that remove wellbeing support
  • imposed silence that blocks active learning, so noise zones should be planned for the learning that needs to happen
  • Multiliteracies (pp. 6–8): multilingualism plus multimodality. The guide asks whether the library is resourced in staffing, space and equipment for multimodal learning, and who is responsible for technology integration. The media capture rig is a multimodality tool.
  • Language profiles (pp. 7–8): personal, functional and instructional languages. The field collection should include home and community languages.
  • Libraries and inquiry (p. 9):
  • Inquiry is initiated by the learner.
  • The IB names curriculum, social-emotional, service, experiential and play-based inquiry.
  • The guide cites the Library of Things model of lending equipment. That is a direct precedent for a lendable mobile lab kit.
  • Librarian archetypes (pp. 14–19):
  • Teacher librarian: co-plans units; supports the PYP exhibition and MYP personal project.
  • Media specialist: technology literacy; may teach MYP design.
  • Designer librarian: designs physical and virtual learning spaces and involves students in co-creating them.
  • Student life librarian: pastoral care and safe spaces.
  • Super librarian (caution): the over-extended librarian. If the library runs the mobile lab, the role needs a clear mandate, time and support, not another add-on.
  • Conclusion (p. 12): the library/ian should be represented in curriculum planning. Effective libraries come from purposeful collaboration between leaders, teachers, learners and library staff.

The library as host or operator of the mobile lab

Three possible models, which can also be phases:

  1. Library as lender (Library of Things). The library catalogues and circulates the kit; teachers and students check it out. The library's caretaking and curating roles fit naturally here.
  2. Library as co-designer. A designer or teacher librarian co-plans field units with teachers, curates the field collection and teaches documentation and research skills on site.
  3. Library as satellite. The field classroom is a distributed branch of the library for its duration: collection, quiet and noisy zones, a documentation wall and pastoral care. This matches the IB's distributed model (p. 3).

Leadership implication: whichever model, the IB warns against the super librarian. Ownership, time, budget and backup staffing must be explicit in the action plan.

Rough daily power budget (to verify against spec sheets):

Load Approx. daily use
10 iPads, full recharge ~300 Wh
1–2 laptops / Mac hub ~150–250 Wh
Satellite terminal (portable model, ~25–40 W, run 8 h) ~200–320 Wh
Wi-Fi access point and switch (~15–20 W, 10 h) ~150–200 Wh
LED projector (~100–150 W, 2 h) ~200–300 Wh
Lights, mics, recorders, misc. ~150 Wh
Total ~1.2–1.5 kWh/day

So the MVP needs about 2 kWh of storage plus 600–800 W of solar to keep a margin through a cloudy day. A full-size satellite terminal running all day roughly doubles its own share of the budget.

Earlier brainstorm notes (retained for reference)

Baseline infrastructure for remote sites. Lean, modular setups have replaced power-hungry desktop labs: LEO satellite terminals, outdoor wireless mesh, offline-first laptops and tablets, software simulation in place of fragile physical gear (e.g. Cisco Packet Tracer), offline content mirrors (offline Wikipedia, Khan Academy), solar plus battery, and passive cooling. See the World Bank's framework for physical learning environments.

Apple media rig gaps. The Apple setup needs:

  • local content caching and MDM, so updates aren't pulled over satellite
  • AirPlay receivers for the projector and screen
  • an audio interface to route wireless mics
  • a dedicated Wi-Fi 6 access point rather than the satellite router
  • local NAS/SSD scratch storage for video
  • a line-interactive UPS, because a short flicker restarts the satellite terminal and costs minutes of re-acquisition

3D model, tech inventory and field sites

The interactive model is this repository's index.html, published with GitHub Pages. It has three tabs.

3D model. The default view is the classroom only. "Whole site" adds the 40 × 40 ft covered area, lockers, outdoor chalkboard, mobile tables and two walk-in pop-up museum cubes (string-grid walls and ceiling for clipped displays). Inside the room:

  • Hanging wipe boards: 76 boards in landscape, each 1′6″ wide × 1′ tall, on four rails.
  • Projection modes:
  • standard
  • a central pull-down screen over the whiteboard, floor to ceiling, fed by a clip-in throw projector
  • center + window screens
  • the whole room as one projected display for an immersive exhibit
  • Ceiling tech grid:
  • drop projectors (any wall can be a projection space)
  • directional mics for recording the room or video calls
  • pull-down power drops
  • clip lights
  • Starlink on the top rail.
  • Brought in as needed: hanging room dividers (wipe board on both sides) and two 75″ rolling screens.
  • Formations:
  • groups
  • two groups (two hexagons)
  • whole group (one long joined table)
  • campfire (any wall)
  • water holes
  • caves: students spread out at tables, at the wall boards, in front of the projection and at the outside chalkboard
  • Student figures: translucent figures that can be switched on.
  • Lesson setups: only what the lesson needs goes on the tables.

Tech inventory. The kit runs from Level 0, where there is no power, to Level 4, a connected room. Each item lists its quantity, purpose, where it lives and when it is used.

Field sites. Illustrated mockups (not simulations) of the kit deployed at the nearby farm, waterfall, river, horse ranch, mountain and mangrove beach. Each gives a provocation, formation, kit, connection, IB theme and safety notes.

Sizes not given in the brief are placeholders and are marked * in the model.
